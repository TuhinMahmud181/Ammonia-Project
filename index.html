    What this simulation is

    • A lumped dynamic model of the rig in PID-002 (simplified): H₂, N₂ and Ar cylinders with regulators; FIC-101 / FIC-102 mass flow controllers and FI-104 rotameter; check valves; the H₂ line to sparger S2 and the N₂/Ar header to sparger S1 or the headspace; R-201 (SS316L, about 0.8 L) with an MgO crucible in the furnace H-201; PSV-301; the outlet needle valve NV-401; the vacuum pump P-601 with its CV-505 bypass; the empty suck-back bottle SB-506, two dilute H₂SO₄ traps AB-507 / AB-508 and the oil bubbler BB-509, venting outdoors.
    • Each gas volume is a well-mixed node holding H₂, N₂, Ar, NH₃ and O₂ as ideal gas. Flows are driven by pressure difference through valve conductances, with bubbler liquid heads, the melt's hydrostatic head at the sparger tips, one-way check valves, and a capped explicit step so any valve line-up stays stable.
    • Heat: the furnace (1.4 kW) runs on its own controller; the reactor and melt lag it by about 10 minutes and sit a little cooler. The melt is solid below 125 °C (DSC onset), mushy to the liquidus, and only lets gas through the spargers when liquid. Headspace gas heats with the melt, so a sealed reactor pressurises as it warms.
    • Chemistry is the unknown the rig is built to measure, so it is a choice, not a prediction. A: YbN forms and is too stable to give NH₃ (the thermodynamic result). B: catalytic NH₃ at a turnover frequency you set, with an Arrhenius term and the equilibrium limit. C: reactive N builds up in the melt under N₂ + H₂ and comes out as NH₃ when the pressure drops (the reported observation). Any O₂ that gets in oxidises the hot Yb.
    • NH₃ capture in the acid falls off at high gas flow and when the acid is used up. The indophenol result carries about 3 % noise and a 0.02 mg N/L detection limit; the page also reports the smallest turnover frequency this run could have seen.
    • Hazards from the HAZOP draft are live: acid suck-back, reverse flow through a stopped pump, H₂ with air, feeding into a solid melt, a sparger freezing shut after a power cut, over-pressure and PSV lift, opening a hot reactor, and a failed leak-up test.

    All set points, volumes and rate constants are provisional design values. Use the page to rehearse the procedure, check valve line-ups, train operators, and see what each kinetic hypothesis would look like in PT-301 and in the acid traps. It does not predict the real kinetics; the measured data will.
